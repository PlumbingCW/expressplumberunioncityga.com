Emergency Sewer Line Repair in Union City, GA

Emergency sewer line repair in Union City, GA begins with finding out why your pipe failed, because red clay, tree roots and age each call for a different fix. Our licensed plumbers camera the line, explain your repair options and give a clear price before any digging starts.

Why sewer lines fail in this part of Georgia

Union City sits on Piedmont red clay. The USDA describes the typical upland soil of this region as sandy loam over a red clay subsoil, with roughly 45 to 55 inches of rain a year. Clay holds moisture around a buried pipe and shifts as it wets and dries, so joints can loosen. Loose joints are the doorway roots use, which is why a camera is the first step on most repair calls.

The city maintains about 91 miles of public sewer, and your private line runs from the house to the connection at that main. In 2006 Union City annexed 5,330 acres to the west and nearly doubled its land area. Because of that growth, the age of your street does not reliably tell you the age or material of your pipe. A recording of the inside tells you more than a neighborhood guess.

Roots follow warm moisture to cracks and loose joints and push inside. Clay tile, which was common in sewer laterals through the late 1980s, is especially easy for them to enter, and PVC with tight joints resists best. Knowing the pipe material changes the repair advice, so it is the second thing we read on the screen.

When a sewer problem is an emergency

  • Sewage is rising in a tub, shower or floor drain, especially when you run the washer or flush a toilet.
  • Several fixtures drain slowly at the same time, and a plunger on one makes no difference to the others.
  • You smell sewage in the yard or crawlspace, or the grass over the line is soggy, sunken or unusually green.
  • A cleaning was done recently and the line backed up again within days.
  • A cleanout cap is wet or has water standing at the top.

Stop using water in the house and call (770) 214-4545. A line that has already failed carries every flush and shower toward the weak spot, so reducing water use cuts the mess while you wait.

A sewer backup is a symptom, not a diagnosis

A backup means wastewater has nowhere to go. The cause may be a blockage, a belly (a sag that collects water and waste), a collapsed section, an offset joint or a root mass. Cleaning clears the symptom, but repair only comes into play when the camera shows damage the pipe cannot recover from.

The usual sequence is simple. We clear the blockage enough to allow a camera through, record the whole run from the cleanout to the main, and mark the damage with a locator. If the pipe is sound, you leave with a clean line and the recording. If it is not, you get the repair options with their trade-offs.

Our sewer camera inspection covers the process in detail. A backup that returns after cleaning almost always means a structural defect, and the video shows where.

Spot repair, pipe lining or replacement

Not every damaged sewer line needs to be dug up. Three paths are common, and the right one depends on the damage, the depth and what sits above the pipe. The table sets them side by side.

Sewer repair options and honest trade-offs
OptionBest forTrade-offs
Spot repair (dig and replace a section)One short failed section, such as a crushed joint or a root-damaged lengthNeeds excavation at that spot, and the rest of the old pipe stays in service
Cured-in-place liningCracks, root-prone joints and worn pipe that is still round and in placeCannot add size, needs the pipe to be sound enough to host a liner, and cuts off use for several hours during cure
Full replacementCollapsed, bellied, badly offset or crumbling pipe over a long runMost disruption, trench or pipe bursting, but gives new pipe from end to end
Cleaning onlySound pipe with a blockageDoes not fix cracks or root entry points, so problems can return

Lining works like this. A flexible liner soaked in resin is pulled or inflated into the old pipe, pressed against the wall, and cured with heat or ultraviolet light. Crews then reopen any side connections it covered. The city of Portland describes it as requiring little or no digging, and notes it cannot upsize a pipe.

Lining is not magic. A pipe that has collapsed or lost its round shape cannot hold a liner, and a deep belly that holds water remains a belly after lining. We tell you plainly when digging is the better value.

Cast iron drain pipe in older Union City homes

Homes built before about 1970 often have cast iron drain and sewer pipe, and trade sources put its service life at roughly 75 to 100 years. Thick walls help it survive, but it corrodes from the inside. Rust builds up and roughens the surface, and wastewater can etch a channel along the bottom that eventually leaks or collapses.

Cast iron problems often show up as slow drains that keep returning, rust-stained water at cleanouts, or a damp spot near a slab. A camera tells us whether the walls are thick enough to line or whether a section should be replaced. If the structure is sound, lining is an option. If it has failed, replacement is the answer.

If you are buying an older home, a camera look at the cast iron before closing is worth more than any guess. It can change what you offer or what you ask the seller to fix.

Septic systems versus the city sewer

The city runs its own sewer, but a property that is not connected to it relies on a septic system, and the repair path is different. If your bill shows no sewer charge or you have a buried tank, ask which one you have. In Fulton County, the Board of Health Environmental Health Services permits and inspects onsite sewage management systems and requires plan review when a new system is proposed.

The College Park Health Center, at (770) 520-7201, is the office listed for Environmental Health services in this part of the county. We repair the house-side pipe between the building and the tank. Tank design, drain field work and new installs go through that office first, so call them before you commit to a replacement plan.

Digging, locating and permits

Georgia law asks everyone who digs, including homeowners, to notify 811 before breaking ground. Georgia 811 states the notice should go in at least two business days before the work begins. Utility crews then mark gas, electric, water and telecom lines, so a trench does not hit one. If your repair needs a trench, we plan the dig around that locate window.

The city lists permits as required for construction, alteration and repair, and issues them to certified contractors. Permit rules vary by job, so ask Union City Community Development at (770) 515-7920 which approval applies to your repair. Part of our work is confirming that before a trench is opened.

Routine clearing is a separate service

If your line is sound and only needs clearing, our sewer line cleaning service is the right visit. Repair is for lines the camera shows are damaged.

What to do while you wait for the crew

  1. Stop all water use. Do not flush, run the dishwasher or start a load of laundry.
  2. Check whether the backup is only at the lowest fixtures. That is the sign the main line is blocked or broken.
  3. Move valuables off the floor in the lowest bathroom, laundry room or basement.
  4. Take dated photos for your landlord or insurer.
  5. Keep children and pets out of any area where sewage has reached the floor, and wear gloves for cleanup.

Permits and digging

Repair or replacement of a building sewer is regulated work. Georgia's plumbing code is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Union City handles permit questions through Community Development at (770) 515-7920. Georgia 811 also has to mark buried utilities before any digging starts.

What changes the price of sewer line repair

Depth and length of damage

A short failed section near the cleanout is a smaller job than a long run under a driveway. Depth also changes how much digging a spot repair needs.

Repair method

Spot repair, lining and full replacement are priced very differently.

What sits above the pipe

Trees, driveways, patios and fences can all raise restoration needs. Lining or pipe bursting can avoid some of them.

Pipe material and condition

Cast iron, clay tile and PVC each behave differently, and a pipe in poor shape may not accept a liner.

Permits and locates

Utility locates and permit steps add time before a dig. Emergency access can sometimes begin earlier, as the situation allows.

Sewer line repair is often treated as wear and tear, which many policies exclude. Sudden damage from a backup into your living space may be covered under a water or sewer backup endorsement. Ask your insurer before work starts, and keep the camera recording because it supports the claim.
