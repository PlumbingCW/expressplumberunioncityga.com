Red clay, slow leaks and the hidden meter test

Palmetto sits in the Georgia Piedmont, where the typical soil is a sandy loam over red clay subsoil. USDA describes the Cecil series as well drained, with roughly 45 to 55 inches of annual rainfall in its range. For a homeowner, that means a leak under a yard may soak down into clay and never surface as a puddle.

A quick meter check separates a hidden leak from normal use. Close every faucet and stop the dishwasher, washer and ice maker. Then watch the meter's small leak dial or low-flow triangle for a minute or two.

  • If the dial spins with every fixture off, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.
  • Run a dye test in each toilet tank first, since a silent flapper leak keeps the meter moving.
  • Look for a patch of grass that stays green or soggy in dry weather along the line from meter to house.
  • Listen at the water heater and under sinks for a faint hiss.
  • If the dial keeps moving after those checks, call us and ask about leak detection.

Burst pipes in a house, not just a hard freeze

Cold snaps are the headline cause. In January 2024, metro Atlanta saw burst pipes and water main breaks, and a master plumber told local news that Georgia homes generally have little insulation. For Palmetto homeowners, that points to pipes in attics, crawlspaces and exterior walls as the likely first failures.

Freezing is only one cause. Corroded copper, a cracked CPVC fitting, a loose washing machine hose and water hammer all split pipes in mild weather too. A burst pipe floods fast, so the first step is always to stop the water, then call. Our burst pipe repair crews cut out the failed section and rejoin it, or replace the run if the metal around it is thin.

When the water heater is the leak

A typical residential tank is 40 to 50 gallons, and when its steel shell rusts through, it empties onto the floor. Leaks at the top fittings or the temperature and pressure relief valve can sometimes be fixed. Water coming from the bottom or the seams of the tank cannot, and replacement is the honest answer.

Warning signs before the failure include rumbling from sediment, rust-colored hot water, a lukewarm shower and a damp floor under the unit. If your heater sits in a closet or attic, shutting off its cold supply valve limits the damage while you call. See water heater replacement for the sizing and fuel questions we walk through.

Permit rules for a water heater swap vary by city. Ask the City of Palmetto Building office at (770) 463-3377 whether your address needs one before the installer starts.

Checking a plumber's license in Palmetto

The Georgia Secretary of State licenses plumbers through the State Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers. Class I Master covers single-family or up to two-family dwellings and commercial work up to 10,000 square feet. Class II Master has no such restriction. That distinction matters when you hire for a home versus a larger building.

Anyone can check a license by name in the state's licensee search before work starts. Do it before you sign, not after, and ask your contractor to confirm who pulls any permit. If you rent, send the same steps to your landlord.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak in Palmetto

Water damage grows by the minute, and a few quick moves in the first stretch of a leak keep it smaller.

Step 1

Find your meter and main

Locate the main shutoff valve inside the house, often near the water heater or where the line enters the wall. If it will not turn, find the meter box at the street and ask the utility how to shut it off safely.

Step 2

Open the low taps

After the main is closed, open the lowest faucet in the house and the highest. This drains the pipes and relieves pressure so the split stops spraying.

Step 3

Kill power near the water

If water is reaching outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off the breaker only if you can reach it without standing in water. Otherwise stay out and tell us when you call.

Step 4

Move belongings and photograph

Lift rugs, electronics and boxes off the floor, then photograph the damage for your insurer. Keep towels at doorways, and keep your phone handy for the dispatcher.
