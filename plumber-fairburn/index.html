Four building eras inside one Fairburn ZIP

Fairburn has not grown evenly. The 1870 census counted 305 people. By 1950 the town had 1,889, and by 2000 it had 5,464. Then the 2010 census recorded 12,950, so a large share of Fairburn homes dates from the 2000s.

That history sorts into four layers, and each layer has its own plumbing habits. If you know the layer, you can guess whether a call is likely to be a tired tank, a root-choked line or a fitting that was never quite right. The table below is a starting point, not a verdict on any one house.

Subdivisions that grew in the 2000s boom

Growth from 5,464 residents in 2000 to 12,950 in 2010 came mostly from new subdivisions. St Johns Crossing was built between 2002 and 2004, according to real estate records. Farnsborough West, north of Ridge Road, covers homes from 1977 to 2001. If your home is in either, it is old enough that the first or second water heater has likely worn out.

Rose Crest townhomes date from 2006 and later, and Seymour Estates has homes from 2016 onward. Newer does not mean trouble free. Tank heaters in a closet or garage still fail at any age, so a water heater repair visit often starts with the burner, thermostat or leaking drain valve.

  • Rust-colored hot water only, or a puddle at the base of the tank, usually means the tank itself is failing and replacement is the honest answer.
  • No hot water at all in a gas unit often points to the pilot assembly or thermocouple. In an electric unit it points to a heating element or thermostat.
  • A rumbling or popping tank is sediment baked onto the bottom. A flush can help if the tank is otherwise sound.
  • If the heater is old and the repair quote is close to the price of a new tank, ask about water heater replacement before you pay for the repair.

Drains in these subdivisions follow a pattern too. Grease in kitchen lines and hair in tub drains cause most slow-drain calls, and a cable machine through the cleanout clears them. A drain that backs up in more than one fixture points toward the main, and that is a different job. Our drain cleaning service covers both cases.

Downtown Fairburn and the old Campbell County seat

Fairburn became the Campbell County seat in 1871 after Campbellton refused to let the Atlanta and West Point Railroad pass through. The old courthouse built that year still stands on West Broad Street. Campbell County was folded into Fulton County in 1932, which is why a Fulton County city has a courthouse that no longer works as one.

The Fairburn Commercial Historic District was added to the National Register in 1988. It includes about 20 commercial buildings and the two depots, both rebuilt in 1917. Buildings of that age were plumbed long before modern fittings existed, so a storefront or older house near Broad Street deserves a camera look before you buy, lease or remodel. Our sewer camera inspection shows the line condition without digging.

Worth knowing for downtown buyers and tenants

The depots were turned into restaurants, and a farmers market and stage now sit in the downtown courtyard. Food service puts grease into drains every day, so shared lines in older commercial blocks fill faster than a home line does.

If you manage a storefront here, a scheduled line check beats an emergency. Our commercial plumbing team works with owners and tenants on that.

What comes out of a Fairburn tap

The City of Fairburn does not run its own treatment plant. Its yearly water quality report says the source is the Chattahoochee River, treated by the City of Atlanta at the Chattahoochee and Hemphill plants. Water reaches Fairburn through 19 master meters, and the city distributes it from there. Because the supply is Atlanta water, the city report is mostly a record of Atlanta results plus the city's own tap sampling.

The 2025 report lists chlorine at an average of 1.13 ppm, with a range from not detected to 1.90 ppm, and fluoride at 0.74 ppm. The report does not list hardness or pH. We will not guess at a number for your street. If scale on fixtures or a crusty heater element worries you, buy a hardness test strip and test your tap.

The same report gives lead advice. Flush your pipes for several minutes after the water has sat, and know that boiling does not remove lead. The utility manager takes lead testing questions at (770) 964-2244, extension 351. Tell us the year your home was built when you call, because pipe age shapes what we check first.

Permits and inspections in Fairburn

Fairburn runs its own Building Division at 314 NW Broad Street, open Monday through Friday from 8:30 am to 4 pm. The city says that work done by a licensed trade person needs a permit, and only minor repairs by the homeowner are exempt. Its forms page lists a combined electric, mechanical and plumbing permit application and a Mechanical Replacement Process sheet.

Inspections are called in between 8 am and 3 pm on weekdays, and the city says all inspections are next-day. Because a permit decides when work can be signed off, tell us early if you plan to replace a water heater or reroute a line. Permit rules vary by job, so confirm yours with the Building Division at (770) 964-2244, extension 401.

Georgia plumbers must be licensed by the state. You can verify any plumber by name in the Secretary of State licensee search before you let them start work.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak in Fairburn

Fairburn homes take water from the city system, so you have two shutoffs to think about. Work through these four steps while you wait for help.

Step 1

Close the house main first

Look for the main valve where the line enters the house, often near the water heater, in a garage or at an exterior wall. A quarter-turn ball valve closes with a quarter turn. An older round gate valve needs several full turns to the right.

Step 2

Find the street meter if it will not close

Fairburn homes sit on city meters. If the house valve is stuck, find the meter box near the curb and call the city at (770) 964-2244 about shutting off at the meter. Do not force a valve that is frozen with age.

Step 3

Kill power to a leaking tank heater

A leaking water heater in a 2000s home is the most common call here. Turn an electric unit off at the breaker. Turn a gas unit's control to pilot or off, then close the cold supply line on top of the tank.

Step 4

Protect floors and photograph the damage

Move rugs and electronics, then open cabinet doors under sinks so air reaches the wet wood. Take photos of wet areas and the source before cleanup, because your insurer will ask for them. Stay out of rooms with water near outlets.
