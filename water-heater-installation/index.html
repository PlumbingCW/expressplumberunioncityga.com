How the day goes, from the first shutoff to the first hot tap

  1. The plumber walks the site, checks the old unit's fuel, size, and venting, and confirms the plan for the new one.
  2. Power or gas goes off, the cold supply is closed, and the old tank is drained to a floor drain or the yard through a hose.
  3. The old heater is disconnected, the water lines and vent are separated, and the tank is moved out.
  4. The new unit is set in a drain pan, leveled, and connected to new flex lines or copper with shutoff valves and a dielectric fitting where metals differ.
  5. Gas lines are connected and leak-checked, or the electric circuit is wired to the unit. Vent pipe is rebuilt to the maker's required slope and clearances.
  6. The relief valve gets its discharge pipe, the tank fills with the hot taps open to bleed air, and then the power or gas is restored.
  7. The plumber tests temperature at a tap, checks for leaks and combustion safety, and hauls the old tank away.

A swap of the same fuel and size in a clear closet is a matter of hours rather than days. Conversions, relocated heaters, and tight attics take longer. The plumber should tell you the scope at the walkthrough so there is no surprise at the end.

Before the crew comes, what to prep

Clear a three-foot path from the door to the heater and move stored boxes off the floor around it. Tell everyone when the water will be off, since hot and cold both go down during the swap. Know where your main shutoff is and make sure it turns, because a stuck main valve is a common delay.

  • Find the data label on the old tank and note fuel type, capacity, and age.
  • Check for a floor drain nearby and a safe place to run the drain hose.
  • For a rental, get written approval from the landlord so the work can begin when the plumber arrives.
  • Plan for a few hours without hot water and bring in bottled water for drinking if needed.

Code items that belong on every install

The relief valve is the final safety device on the tank. Watts says residential valves are typically rated to open at 150 psi and 210 degrees Fahrenheit, and the discharge pipe must be the valve's size and slope downward to a safe place. The plumber should never cap, plug, or reduce that line.

An expansion tank handles thermal expansion in a closed system. A check valve or backflow preventer on the supply blocks expanding water from flowing back, so pressure builds in the tank. The Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District explains that the expansion tank is the second safety device, and a dripping relief valve is often the first sign you need one.

A drain pan with a pipe to a safe drain protects a ceiling or finished floor below the heater. Union City's permit page lists the 2018 International Plumbing Code and International Fuel Gas Code with Georgia amendments. Permit rules vary, so ask Union City Community Development at (770) 515-7920 how a swap is handled.

Sizing the unit and fitting it to the household

The Department of Energy sizes storage heaters by first hour rating, matched to your busiest hour. Add up showers, dishwasher, and laundry in that hour and pick a unit that meets or exceeds the total. Their worked example comes to 66 gallons for three showers, a shave, and handwashed dishes.

A Union City household averages roughly 2.47 people, according to Census QuickFacts, but averages hide the big family and the single renter. Tell the plumber who lives there and the hour everyone bathes. That detail is more useful than the square footage.

Hauling the old tank and what comes after

Even a drained tank is bulky and catches on doorframes, stairs, and tight turns. The crew removes it and takes it away so it does not sit in your garage. Ask at the walkthrough whether haul-away is on the written quote.

After the install, keep the manual and register the warranty. Set a yearly reminder to check the relief valve for drips and the area for moisture. A quick glance at the pan and the connections can catch a small problem before it grows.

Rentals and Union City particulars

Owners occupy only 40.4 percent of Union City homes, so many installs are ordered by a landlord rather than the person who lives there. If that is your building, ask whether the plumber can coordinate with the property manager. A tenant should never sign off on a new tank without the owner's approval.

The city buys treated water from Atlanta and does not list hardness in its quality report. Test your tap before you choose a tank, and ask the water department at (770) 306-6855 for the report. If your water is hard, scale will shorten the life of any heater, so a plan for flushing belongs in the decision.

What changes the price of a water heater installation

Type and capacity

A conventional 40-gallon tank, a larger family tank, a heat pump unit, and a tankless unit all sit at different price points.

Fuel and utilities

A gas line that needs resizing or a new 240-volt circuit adds parts and labor to the job.

Venting and code work

New flue pipe, an expansion tank, a drain pan, and a relief valve discharge line are common additions.

Location and access

A garage is easier than an attic or crawl space. Moving a heater to a new spot adds supply, drain, and vent changes.

Permit and haul-away

The permit process and the removal of the old tank are part of the scope. Ask what your quote includes.

A planned installation is a maintenance or upgrade expense, so homeowner insurance usually does not pay for it. If an old tank has already flooded the house, the water damage may be covered as sudden and accidental, so call your insurer first. Ask your landlord for approval if you rent.
