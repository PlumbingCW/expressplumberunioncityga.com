Fall rain and the sewer line in Riverdale

In late September 2024, Tropical Storm Helene dumped radar-estimated 10 to 12 inches of rain on Clayton County, according to the National Weather Service. The Flint River gauge near Lovejoy crested at a record major flood. Across the county, floodwater reached homes along Roxbury Drive, Rambo Court and Mockingbird Road, plus businesses near Upper Riverdale Road and Gardenwalk Boulevard. Low streets deserve a drain check after every hard fall rain.

CCWA lists heavy rainfall as one cause of sewer backups, because the public system can overload and push wastewater back toward houses. If your lowest fixture sits below the next upstream manhole, CCWA says you need a sanitary backflow device on your lateral. Without one, cleanup falls on you. Ask a plumber to confirm that device is on your lateral before the next big rain, not after.

Smaller warning signs show up first. A tub that gurgles when the washer drains, a floor drain that smells, or a toilet that bubbles all point to a restriction downstream. A cable machine can clear a grease or paper clog in a branch line, and a camera shows whether roots or a broken joint caused it. A visit for drain cleaning usually starts with the cable and the camera before anyone suggests anything bigger.

When sewage is coming up

Stop using water in the whole house, including the washer and dishwasher. Every gallon you run has nowhere to go but the lowest drain.

Keep children and pets away from the wet area. Then call us, because raw sewage on a floor is an emergency, not a next-week job.

The Christmas 2022 freeze and what it broke

Sub-freezing weather around Christmas 2022 froze and burst pipes across metro Atlanta. Clayton County went under a state of emergency, and roughly 5,000 to 6,000 customers had low pressure or no water for days. County officials named Riverdale among the hardest-hit areas. CCWA also said several apartment complexes and mobile home parks stayed low because the breaks sat on private property, so owners and landlords should know their own shutoff.

CCWA's own freeze guidance names the usual weak points. Hose bibs, sprinkler lines, and supply pipes in crawl spaces, attics, garages and kitchen cabinets freeze first, along with pipes in poorly insulated exterior walls. On very cold nights it suggests dripping a faucet on exposed lines, opening cabinet doors, and holding the thermostat steady. If you leave town, keep the heat at 55 degrees or higher.

  • Disconnect and drain garden hoses, then close the inside valve to each hose bib and leave the outside spigot open.
  • Wrap pipes in a crawl space, garage or attic with foam sleeves, or heat cable that carries a UL listing.
  • Learn where the main shutoff is today, because CCWA notes it varies by house, and slab homes often keep it near the water heater.
  • Let a cold tap drip on the coldest night, preferably at the faucet farthest from where the line enters the house.

If only a trickle comes out, CCWA says to suspect a frozen pipe near an exterior wall or where the service line enters the foundation. Warm it with a heating pad or hair dryer, never a blowtorch or open flame. If it will not thaw, call a licensed plumber. A split pipe is a job for burst pipe repair, and the main should stay shut until then.

Spring hydrant flushing and the water quality report

CCWA publishes its Water Quality Report every spring. The 2026 edition covers 2025 testing and says the system draws from three primary watersheds, Little Cotton Indian Creek, Shoal Creek and Pates Creek, with the Flint River as a supplement. The report gives no hardness number to quote here, so test your tap with a kit or call CCWA at (770) 960-5200 before sizing a softener or judging scale in a tank.

CCWA and the county fire service also flush hydrants through the year. Nearby homes can see a short pressure drop and brown or cloudy water, which CCWA describes as mostly silt and air. It asks residents to skip laundry during the flush, then wait about ten minutes and run cold taps until the water clears. A brief brown spell after flushing is rarely a plumbing failure, so wait before booking a repair.

Brown water that shows up on the hot side only, long after any flushing, is different. That pattern points to sediment or corrosion inside the water heater tank, and it is worth a look before the tank starts leaking.

Summer bills, the meter dial, and a water heater near the end

CCWA gives a simple leak test that works in any month. Shut off every tap and appliance, record the meter reading, wait 15 to 20 minutes, and read it again. A triangular dial on the meter spins even at very low flow. If the number moved with everything off, water is escaping after the meter, which is the homeowner's side of the line, and a leak detection visit can pinpoint the pipe.

Water heaters are the other quiet summer cost. Riverdale grew from 1,045 residents in 1960 to 12,478 in 2000, so many homes here were built decades ago and may still hold their second or third tank. A tank that weeps at the base or rusts at the fittings is usually past repair, while a dead element or a bad thermostat often is not. A good plumber explains that split before quoting a water heater replacement.

Replacement is also a permit question here. The City of Riverdale says plumbing, electrical and mechanical work needs a permit, though its page does not list water heaters by name. Ask the city office at (770) 909-5486 before the old tank comes out, so the new one passes inspection the first time.

Where CCWA's pipes end and yours begin

CCWA maintains water service up to and including the meter, which usually sits in a box near the curb. Everything after the meter belongs to the property owner. If you see water running down a street, CCWA asks you to call it right away at (770) 960-5200 so it can check for a main or service line break.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak in Riverdale

These four steps limit damage while you wait. Do only what you can do safely, and call us first if water is near electrical outlets.

Step 1

Find the home valve, not the curb box

CCWA says the valve location varies, but slab houses often keep it near the water heater and crawl space houses keep it under the floor. If the home valve will not turn, CCWA can be reached at (770) 960-5200 about the meter.

Step 2

Kill power to the heater

If water is pooling around the tank, flip its breaker or set the gas control to off. A heater firing with a broken supply can overheat or burn out its elements.

Step 3

Stay out of the lowest drains

If the problem is a sewer backup, do not flush, run the washer or wash dishes. Keep shoes on and keep kids away from the floor.

Step 4

Photograph before you mop

Take clear photos of the damage and the wet walls for your insurer. CCWA warns that typical homeowner policies do not always cover sewer backups, so ask your agent.
