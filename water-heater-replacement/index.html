Repair or replace, the decision in one table

Most people ask one question first. Is this heater worth fixing? The honest answer depends on five things you can check yourself in about ten minutes, starting with the data label on the side of the tank.

Repair or replace, by what you find
What you findPoints toward repairPoints toward replacement
Age on the data labelYoung tank with a single failed partOld tank, or one near the end of its warranty
Where the water isFittings, relief valve, drain valve, element gasketSeeping from the base or through the jacket

A repair is smart when one cheap part has failed on an otherwise sound tank. Replacement wins when the leak is in the steel, because there is no patch for corroded metal. The water heater repair page covers the fixable symptoms in detail.

When a failed tank is an emergency

A tank that leaks from its body can release dozens of gallons onto a floor, and some fail suddenly. Call the same hour if water is spreading, if the relief valve is discharging hot water, or if the heater sits above living space or finished drywall. A slow drip into a pan is less urgent, but it will not stop on its own.

  1. Turn off the electric breaker, or turn the gas control knob to off.
  2. Close the cold-water shutoff valve on the line above the tank. If it will not turn, close the main shutoff at the house.
  3. Move rugs, boxes, and electronics out of the water's path and keep people away from standing water near outlets.
  4. Photograph the flooding and the data label for the landlord or insurer before you mop.
  5. Call (770) 214-4545 and give the fuel type and tank size so the right unit is on the truck.

If water has reached drywall, carpet, or an adjoining room, tell your insurer promptly. Sudden tank failure is often treated differently from slow, long-term leaks, so the date and photos matter. A leak detection visit is also worth considering if the damage seems larger than the tank could explain.

No hot water, and the heater is old

A cold shower on an old tank sometimes means a dead element or thermocouple and sometimes means the end of the line. A plumber can test the element and gas valve in minutes, then price the repair beside the cost of a new unit. If the tank is past its warranty and also shows rust or sediment, many owners choose to stop paying for repairs.

Sizing the new heater for the household

The Department of Energy sizes storage and heat pump tank heaters by first hour rating. That is the number of gallons the unit can deliver in the first hour starting full. You add up your busiest hour of use, such as three showers at 60 gallons together, one shave, and one round of dishes, and pick a rating at or above the total.

Union City households average about 2.47 people according to Census QuickFacts. That fits a smaller tank in an apartment or condo, but a house with teenagers and a laundry habit is a different job. Tell the plumber who lives there and when everyone showers.

Bigger is not always better. A tank that is much larger than the household needs stays hot at idle and wastes energy, and it may not fit the closet. A plumber measures the space, checks the old unit's rating, and recommends a match.

Gas, electric, or heat pump, and switching fuels

Fuel options and what each asks of the house
TypeWhat it needsHonest trade-off
Gas storage tankGas line, vent through the roof or wall, combustion airHeats fast but needs a safe vent and gas line in good shape
Electric storage tankA dedicated 240-volt circuit and breakerSimple to install but slower recovery on a large household
Heat pump (hybrid) tank240-volt circuit and 1,000 cubic feet of air at 40 to 90 degreesTwo to three times more efficient than standard electric, but costs more upfront and cools its room

Switching from gas to a heat pump unit is a bigger job than a like-for-like swap. The gas line gets capped, the old flue is removed or sealed, and the electrical panel has to have room for a new 240-volt breaker. The Department of Energy notes that heat pump water heaters cost more upfront, and the lower operating cost can offset the difference over time.

Heat pumps pull heat from room air and so they work best in a warm utility space. A tight, unheated closet will starve one. Your plumber should check the space before recommending it.

Venting, expansion tanks, and drain pans on a swap

A gas heater has to vent exhaust out of the house. Union City's permit page lists the 2018 International Fuel Gas Code with Georgia amendments, so an old flue that is corroded, sagging, or too short is a code question as well as a safety one. Replacing the heater is the time to fix it.

Thermal expansion matters too. When a backflow preventer or check valve blocks water from flowing back to the supply, heated water has nowhere to go and pressure climbs. An expansion tank is a small air-cushioned vessel that absorbs it, and a relief valve that drips each time the heater runs often points to the need for one.

The relief valve needs a discharge pipe that slopes downward to a safe place, according to Watts. A drain pan under a heater in a closet or attic catches slow leaks before they reach the ceiling below. These parts are cheap next to the damage they prevent.

Permit rules vary, so ask Union City Community Development at (770) 515-7920. The city's permit page says permits are issued to the certified contractor, so a homeowner should not assume a handyman can pull one. Ask the company you hire who handles it.

With only 40.4 percent of Union City homes owner-occupied, a failed tank is often a tenant's emergency and a landlord's purchase. A tenant cannot approve a new tank alone. Send the landlord a photo of the leak and the data label, and ask for written approval so the plumber can start.

The city's water comes from Atlanta, and its water quality report does not publish hardness. That means no one can say how quickly scale will build in a new tank. Test your tap and think about whether a tankless unit with its own maintenance routine suits the house better than another storage tank.

What to do before the replacement crew arrives

  • Clear a three-foot path to the heater and remove stored items from around it.
  • Know where the main water shutoff is, and confirm it turns.
  • Find the breaker or gas shutoff for the heater and keep it off.
  • Write down the old unit's fuel type, capacity, and any visible model number.
  • Ask your landlord or insurer who will authorize the work and send them photos.

What changes the price of a water heater replacement

Tank size and fuel type

A 40-gallon electric tank and a larger gas model sit at different price points. Heat pump models cost more upfront than conventional ones.

Venting and gas line condition

Old flue pipe, a missing draft hood, or an undersized gas line adds parts and labor to a gas swap.

Code items

Expansion tanks, relief valve discharge lines, drain pans, and shutoff valves can all be required or recommended on a replacement.

Access and location

A garage floor is faster than an attic or a tight closet. Stairs, narrow doors, and removal add time.

Fuel conversion

Moving from gas to electric or heat pump involves a new circuit and capping the gas line, which is a larger scope of work.

Insurers often cover sudden and accidental water damage when a tank bursts, and less often the heater itself when it wore out. Ask your insurer before the visit. Keep the failed unit's data label and photos of the damage.
