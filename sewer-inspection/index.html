Sewer Camera Inspection in Union City, GA

A sewer camera inspection in Union City, GA shows the inside of your drain or sewer line on a screen, so the repair decision rests on what is actually there. A licensed plumber feeds a waterproof camera through a cleanout, records the run and marks where any problem sits.

What happens when you call

The call is short and practical. A dispatcher asks what you have noticed, such as slow drains, a gurgling toilet, a wet patch in the yard or a home purchase deadline. We also ask where the cleanout is, whether the house is a rental, and whether a cable or jet has been used recently.

Those answers decide what we bring. A recently blocked line may need a cleaning pass before a camera fits through, and a home purchase usually needs a written summary with clear stills. If this is urgent, tell us. We offer same-day appointments when available, and for sewage backups our emergency sewer line cleaning line runs 24/7.

You also get a clear price before work starts. The visit time, the camera length needed and any access work are the main factors, which we cover below.

On site, step by step

  1. The plumber finds the access point. This is usually a cleanout cap outside the house, in a crawlspace or inside a utility room. If there is none, a toilet may be pulled to get in.
  2. The area is protected with drop cloths, and the cap is removed slowly in case water or gas is trapped behind it.
  3. A flexible cable with a camera head goes into the pipe. A self-leveling head keeps the image upright as it rotates, so a crack at the top reads as a crack at the top.
  4. The plumber watches the screen as the cable advances. A footage counter on the reel records the distance to every feature.
  5. At the points that matter, the plumber stops and records a still. That includes joints, roots, grease coating, low spots and breaks.
  6. The run continues to the city connection if the camera length allows, then is pulled back while the recording continues.
  7. You see the findings on the screen before the plumber packs up, with plain answers on what each one means.

A line with heavy debris may need clearing before the camera can pass, and we tell you so before billing.

What a good camera report should show

A recording alone is not enough. The useful report ties each finding to a location, a size and a recommendation. Ask for these items from any company you hire.

  • The pipe material and diameter, such as cast iron, clay, Orangeburg, ABS or PVC, and where the material changes along the run.
  • Footage marks for each finding, measured from the cleanout so a repair crew can find the spot without guessing.
  • Plain labels for what the camera saw, including root intrusion, cracks, offset or separated joints, bellies, scale and grease buildup.
  • Whether water or soil enters through a break, which signals active failure.
  • A recommendation for each issue, whether it is clean, monitor, line or replace.
  • The video file itself, delivered to you, a landlord or a buyer's agent.

The camera also shows what no one can see from above. Roots appear as fine hair-like masses or thicker ropes at joints. A belly looks like a stretch of standing water in the bottom of the pipe. A fracture shows as a dark line with a visible edge.

Pipe locating, sonde and depth

Many camera heads contain a sonde, a small coil that broadcasts a radio signal, commonly at 512 hertz. A handheld receiver above ground picks up that signal. The plumber sweeps the receiver until it peaks, which marks the head's horizontal position, and reads an estimated depth from signal strength.

Depth and position matter because they decide how a repair is done. A pipe four feet down under a driveway is a different job from one eighteen inches below a lawn. Buried metal, electric lines and wet clay can interfere with the reading, so the plumber cross-checks it against the footage counter before marking the ground.

If you plan to dig, whether it is a fence post, a patio or a trench, call Georgia 811 first. The locate we do for your pipe marks the private line only, and it does not replace the utility locate.

Buying a home in Union City? Why to scope the sewer first

A visual home inspection stops at the fixtures. The sewer line between the house and the street is hidden, and a failed line is one of the more expensive surprises after closing. A home is likely the largest purchase you will make, so a repair bill on a buried line is worth checking before you commit to the price.

A camera inspection during the due diligence window gives you a recording and a written summary of the line. If it shows roots, offsets or a collapsed section, you can ask the seller for repairs or a credit, or walk away. If it shows a clean pipe, you have a baseline for future work.

Schedule early. Sellers in a rental or occupied property may need notice, and the cleanout must be reachable. Older homes with cast iron pipe benefit most, since corrosion is hard to judge from a floor drain.

When the camera finds a problem

The next step depends on the finding. Roots at one joint might call for cutting and a spot repair. A long run of scale on cast iron may call for cleaning or lining. A collapsed section means digging. From inspection to repair

An inspection only looks, but the repair it leads to may need a permit, and building sewers fall under the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Ask for the video file and a note of the footage mark where each defect sits, so the repair quote and any permit review refer to the same spot.

What changes the price of a sewer camera inspection

Access to the line

A cleanout makes the visit simple. If there is no cleanout, the crew may have to pull a toilet or find another entry.

Length of the line

A long run to the city main needs more cable and more recording time than a short lateral.

Pipe condition

A clean pipe takes one pass. A line full of debris may need cleaning first, which is a separate service.

Locating and marking

Marking the depth and path of the pipe on the ground for a planned dig adds time and is quoted with the visit.

Reports and timing

A written summary with stills for a buyer, a landlord or an insurer takes more time than a simple recording.

Insurers rarely pay for an inspection that is done only to look. If the camera is part of a claim for sudden damage, such as a backup that ruined a floor, the recording can support it. Ask your insurer which documents they need.
