About Union City Express Plumbing

Union City Express Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing dispatch line serving Union City, Georgia and the towns around it. The office address is 4500 Jonesboro Rd, Union City, GA 30291.

This page explains how a call becomes a visit, what we cover and how you can check any plumber for yourself.

How the dispatch line works

When you call (770) 214-4545, dispatch answers and writes down the address, the symptom and any safety risk. A licensed plumber is then sent to the job. The work itself is done by licensed plumbing professionals, and the price is explained before it starts.

Emergencies are handled first, around the clock. Non-urgent jobs such as a slow drain or a new faucet are booked as same-day appointments when the schedule allows.

Check any plumber's license yourself

Georgia licenses plumbers through the State Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, part of the Secretary of State's office. The division runs a public licensee search where you can look up a plumber by name.

Class I Master licenses cover single-family homes and commercial work up to a size limit, and Class II Master licenses have no such limit. Ask who is being sent and search the name before work begins. A good plumber will not mind.

If a name does not appear in the search, ask the plumber to spell it exactly as it is licensed. If it still does not appear, tell dispatch before any work starts and ask for a different plumber. The search takes about a minute and helps you avoid unlicensed work.

What we cover and what we do not

We cover the services on our plumbing services page, from drain cleaning to water heaters to sewer lines. Permit questions for a job inside the city go to Union City Community Development at (770) 515-7920.

We do not diagnose problems over the phone as a guarantee, and we do not quote a firm number before someone has seen the work. What we do promise is a clear price before any work starts.

Why local detail matters

Union City's water comes from the City of Atlanta through the city's own lines, and its soil is Piedmont red clay. Those two facts shape what fails here and why. Our pages explain them in plain terms, so you can judge a recommendation instead of taking it on faith.

What happens after you hang up

Dispatch confirms the address and the problem, then sends a licensed plumber. The plumber looks at the problem, explains what is wrong in plain words and gives the price. You decide whether to go ahead, and no work begins before that.

If the job needs a permit, the plumber tells you before starting. Union City says permits are issued to the certified contractor through Community Development, so ask the plumber how your job is handled.

What to do while you wait

  1. Close the main water valve, usually near the water heater, in a garage or where the line enters the house.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  3. Move rugs, electronics and valuables away from the water.
  4. Take photos and short videos of the damage for your insurer.
  5. If you smell gas near a water heater, leave the house and call 911 from outside.

These steps limit damage and give the plumber a clear scene to work in.

Local contacts worth saving

Union City Public Services handles city water and sewer questions at (770) 306-6855, with an after-hours extension. Community Development, at (770) 515-7920, answers permit questions and lists the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments on its permit page. Georgia 811 marks buried lines before anyone digs.

Saving these three numbers next to ours means a broken pipe, a billing question and a digging question each reach the right office on the first call.

How the guides on this site are written

Every town, service and guide page is built from primary sources where they exist. That means city and county utility pages, state licensing pages, published water quality reports and Census figures. When a number is not published, such as water hardness in Union City, the page says so and tells you to test your tap.

We do not show an owner name, license details or review totals on this site, because we would rather leave them out than show something we cannot back. Use the Georgia licensee search to check the plumber who actually arrives.

Prices are never printed here, because the real number depends on the pipe, the access, the parts and the hour. Each service page lists the factors that move the price, so the quote you hear makes sense.
