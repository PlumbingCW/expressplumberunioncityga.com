What the city records say about Dixie Lakes

Union City's 2020 plan update names Dixie Lake directly. It proposes to reclaim Dixie Lakes for a zone of community recreational activities and ties the area to a citywide park system.

The same plan notes that an early grade school on Dixie Lake Road stood on land donated by C.H. Gullatt. One photo caption refers to the Dixie Lake Country Club before 1930. The 2015 plan update also shows homes off Lower Dixie Lake Road.

We describe the area by its roads because the plans name the lake and its roads rather than publish a mapped boundary. Union Pointe, a townhouse and single-family community built between 2006 and 2020, sits just west of Lower Dixie Lake Road and has its own homeowners association. It uses the same services and the same number.

Six decades of houses on the same streets

Listing records for Dixie Lakes show homes from 1942 through 2002. Houses that far apart in age were built to different codes and with different materials. That does not mean any one house has a problem. It means you should not assume your plumbing matches your neighbor's.

Plumbing services for older single-family homes

  • A slow drain or repeated clog: drain cleaning clears the line, and a sewer camera inspection shows why it keeps coming back.
  • No hot water or a leaking tank: start with water heater repair. If the tank is old, replacement can make more sense than another repair.
  • Wet spots, a high water bill or low pressure: leak detection finds the source before walls are opened.
  • Rusty water or leaks at several fixtures: ask about repiping so the whole supply side gets looked at once.

Call 811 before you dig

Call Georgia 811 before any digging, including fence posts and garden beds. The call is free, and it keeps buried lines from being hit.

Water, permits and who to call in the city

Union City buys its water from the City of Atlanta. The city's Public Services department takes calls about the local system at (770) 306-6855, so call them first if water is out across the street or you see a leak at the meter.

Work on pipes inside a house can need a permit. Community Development lists the 2018 International Plumbing Code with Georgia amendments on its permit page and answers questions at (770) 515-7920.
