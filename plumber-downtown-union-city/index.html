A small city with a 1908 start

Union City was chartered on August 17, 1908. Drewry Arthur Carmical became the first mayor, and the town was named for the National Farmers Union, which was looking for a headquarters site.

A historic home built about 1910 on Watson Street is now the Green Manor Restaurant. Buildings from that era have been repaired and reused for a century, so the plumbing history of a downtown address is often unknown. A camera inspection answers what old records do not.

Houses around Union Street

The city's 2020 plan describes the residential neighborhoods between US 29 and I-85 as places for infill that respects existing homes, many of which have historic value. Photos in the plan show homes along Westbrook Avenue.

An older house often carries repairs layered over many years. Start with a diagnosis before you decide on a fix.

  • Slow or repeated clogs: drain cleaning, followed by a sewer camera inspection if the problem returns.
  • Hot water trouble: water heater repair, or replacement when the tank is at the end of its life.
  • Stains, damp floors or a high water bill: leak detection.
  • Leaks at several fixtures: ask about repiping.

Small commercial buildings and reused spaces

The city's plan sees tremendous opportunity downtown for housing, retail and services, including adaptive reuse of historic structures. When a space changes use, the plumbing load changes with it.

Water, digging and who to call

Union City buys its water from the City of Atlanta. If water is out for the whole block, call Public Services at (770) 306-6855 before you call a plumber.

Older lots and buried lines

Call Georgia 811 before any digging. Older downtown lots can have buried lines that no longer match anyone's drawings, and the call is free.
