A 24 hour plumber for Union City emergencies

Plumbing problems rarely wait for office hours. A supply line lets go at two in the morning, a water heater tank splits on a Sunday, or a main line backs up the night before guests arrive. That is why this emergency plumber line in Union City never closes.

When you call (770) 214-4545, dispatch asks a few plain questions: what is leaking or blocked, where the shutoff is, and whether anyone is at risk. Then a licensed plumber is sent to your address in Union City or a nearby town, and the price is explained before any work starts.

These are the problems we treat as emergencies:

  • A burst or leaking pipe that is soaking walls, ceilings or floors, covered on our burst pipe repair page.
  • Sewage or gray water backing up into a tub, shower or floor drain, which usually points to a main line problem.
  • A water heater that is leaking from the tank, or a gas water heater with a smell of gas around it.
  • No water at all, or a water meter that keeps spinning when every fixture is off.

Everything else, from a slow drain to a dripping faucet, can be booked as a same-day appointment when the schedule allows. If you are not sure which group your problem falls into, call anyway. Describing the symptom takes a minute and dispatch will tell you what can wait.

Plumbing in Union City: what is different about the pipes here

Union City buys its water from the City of Atlanta, which treats Chattahoochee River water, and then delivers it through the city's own 112 miles of water line. The city also owns about 91 miles of sewer. In practice that means a leak on the street side of your meter is the city's job, while anything past the meter is yours. Public Services at (770) 306-6855 can tell you which side a problem sits on.

The city's water quality report does not publish a hardness figure, so no one can honestly promise how fast scale will build in your water heater. The practical step is to test your tap and flush a tank once a year. A water heater repair visit is much cheaper than an emergency replacement.

Census QuickFacts puts owner-occupied housing in Union City at 40.4 percent, so a large share of homes are rentals. If you rent, tell your landlord about a leak the same day and take photos, because water damage grows by the hour. Landlords and property managers can call the same 24/7 line, and our commercial plumbing page covers small business and multifamily work.

Red clay Piedmont soil shifts as it dries and wets, and that movement stresses buried lines. Roots follow water into cracked sewer joints, which is why repeat clogs near Mayors' Park, along Jonesboro Road or in the neighborhoods off Lakeside Drive often end in a sewer camera inspection. The camera shows whether the fix is a cleaning or a repair.

Cold snaps are rare here, which is exactly why they hurt. The January 2024 freeze burst pipes and water mains across metro Atlanta, and few Georgia homes have much pipe insulation. Know where your main shutoff is before the next one. Before any digging for a line repair, Georgia 811 must mark the buried utilities, and permit questions go to Union City Community Development at (770) 515-7920, whose permit page lists the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments.
