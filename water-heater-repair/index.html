Emergency Water Heater Repair in Union City, GA

Emergency water heater repair in Union City, GA starts with the symptom you can see or hear. No hot water, a pilot that will not stay lit, a rumbling tank, and water pooling on the floor each point to a different part. No hot water at all, and what usually causes it

On a gas heater, a cold tap usually means the burner is out. The pilot may have blown out, the thermocouple may have failed, or the gas control valve may have stopped responding. On an electric heater, the usual suspects are a tripped breaker, a burned-out heating element, or a thermostat that no longer passes power.

A plumber sorts these out with a multimeter instead of guessing. Checking for 240 volts at the element terminals, then testing element resistance with the power off, separates a dead element from a dead thermostat. A gas unit gets a flame and draft check plus a thermocouple reading.

Before you call, look at the breaker panel for a tripped double-pole breaker and look under the tank for water. If you find a puddle, jump to the leak steps below. A tank that leaks from its body cannot be repaired, and every hour of delay adds to the flooding.

Lukewarm water, or hot water that runs out too fast

Lukewarm water usually means half the system is working. An electric tank has an upper and a lower element, and when the lower one fails the top third of the tank still heats. You get a short burst of hot water, then a cold stream.

A cracked dip tube causes a similar complaint. This plastic tube carries cold supply water to the bottom of the tank, and when it breaks the cold water mixes near the outlet. Sediment is the third cause. Minerals settle on the tank floor and insulate the water from the burner or lower element.

Sometimes nothing is broken and the heater is simply too small. The U.S. Department of Energy sizes a storage heater by its first hour rating, which is the gallons it can supply in the first hour from a full tank. Three showers, one shave, and a round of hand dishwashing add up to about 66 gallons, so that household needs a rating of 66 or more.

Pilot light out, burner will not light, or the flame keeps dying

Relighting a standing pilot is a task many homeowners can do by following the label on the tank. If the pilot lights but dies the moment you release the knob, the thermocouple is probably not producing enough current. It is a small part and a routine repair.

If you smell gas

Leave the house first. Call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches, use a lighter, or try to relight anything until a professional has cleared the smell.

Newer gas heaters with electronic ignition have no standing pilot. They fail through a dirty flame sensor, a weak igniter, or a blocked vent that trips a safety switch. A heater that keeps locking out is protecting you, so the venting should be checked before anyone resets it again.

Rumbling, popping, hissing, or ticking from the tank

Popping and rumbling come from sediment. Water trapped under the scale layer flashes into steam bubbles that burst through it, and that is the noise you hear. The scale also overheats the steel at the bottom of the tank, which shortens its life.

A hiss or drip near the top points to the temperature and pressure relief valve. According to Watts, residential valves are typically built to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees Fahrenheit, and a valve that discharges is reporting an unsafe condition. Never cap or plug the valve or its discharge line.

Ticking is often harmless. Copper and plastic lines expand as hot water moves through them, and a loose pipe strap can knock or tick for months. That is a minor visit, not an emergency.

When a water heater problem becomes an emergency

Treat it as an emergency when water is leaking from the tank body, the relief valve is blowing hot water, you smell gas, or you notice a scorched odor near the burner. A cold shower is an inconvenience. Water spreading across a floor or a gas smell needs action now.

  1. Switch off the power at the breaker for an electric heater, or turn the gas control knob to off for a gas heater.
  2. Close the cold-water shutoff valve on the pipe above the tank. A lever that sits crosswise to the pipe is closed.
  3. If water keeps coming, close the main shutoff where the line enters the house, usually near the meter or the front hose bib.
  4. Move belongings off the floor and keep everyone away from standing water near outlets or power strips.
  5. Call (770) 214-4545 and tell the dispatcher the fuel type, the tank size on the label, and where the water is going.

Do not leave an electric heater powered with the tank empty

Heating elements burn out within moments when they are not covered by water. Turn off the breaker before you drain or lose supply to the tank.

Where a leaking tank leaks from, and when repair is off the table

Not every drip means a dead tank. Water can come from the fittings on top, the relief valve, the drain valve, an electric element gasket, or the steel body. The first four are repairs. A leak through the body is not.

A pinhole in the tank cannot be patched safely. Water pressure keeps pushing through the corrosion and the hole only grows. Once the leak is in the steel, replacing the heater is the only real fix, and sooner is better because a full tank holds dozens of gallons.

The sacrificial anode rod hanging inside the tank is built to corrode in place of the tank wall. Bonney notes that a tank without a working rod may last about 8 to 12 years. A fresh rod can slow corrosion on a sound tank, but it cannot close a hole that already exists.

Gas, electric, and hybrid water heater repair, and what fails in each

Gas heaters

The pilot assembly, thermocouple, gas control valve, burner, and flue draft are the common failures. Vent problems matter most because exhaust has to leave the house.

Electric heaters

Upper and lower elements, thermostats, and the high-limit reset button wear out first. Most of these are fast part swaps when the tank is sound.

Hybrid heat pump heaters

A compressor, fan, and control board move heat from the room air into the tank, with electric elements as backup. Sealed system and board repairs are pricier.

The Department of Energy says a heat pump water heater should sit in a space between 40 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit with at least 1,000 cubic feet of air around it. A hybrid squeezed into a small closet can struggle without being broken. Ask whether a better location fixes the complaint before paying for parts.

Age and warranty carry extra weight on hybrids. Before approving a compressor or board repair, check the label for the manufacture date and ask if the manufacturer warranty still applies. For heaters headed to retirement, a tankless unit is another option to price.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

  • Keep children and pets out of the room if water is standing on the floor.
  • Photograph the data label and the leak, since landlords and insurers both ask for the model, serial number, and capacity.
  • Leave the relief valve and its discharge pipe alone, because the water can be scalding.
  • Do not turn the thermostat up to chase hot water, because a failed element or thermocouple will not respond.
  • Avoid running hot taps, and if the tank is wet, keep the breaker off until a plumber has looked at it.

What changes the price of a water heater repair

Which part failed

A thermocouple or relief valve is a small part and a short job. A gas control valve or a hybrid compressor costs far more in parts and time.

Fuel type and model

Electric elements, gas burners, and heat pump systems each need different parts and testing tools, so the same symptom can lead to different bills.

Age and condition of the tank

A tank near the end of its life may not be worth a repair. The plumber should tell you that before starting work.

Access and code items

A tight closet, old venting, or a missing drain pan adds labor. Permit rules vary, so ask Union City Community Development at (770) 515-7920 if a swap or vent change is needed.

Time of the call

Nights, weekends, and holidays follow after-hours rates. You get the clear price before work starts.

Homeowner policies often cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a tank that bursts and floods a floor. They usually do not pay to replace a heater that wore out from age or sediment. Ask your insurer before the visit, and keep photos and the old unit's data label.
