Frozen Pipes in Union City, GA, What to Do and What Never to Do

If you turn on a faucet and only a trickle comes out on a freezing day, suspect a frozen pipe. Leave the faucet open, find the cold section, and warm it gently with a hair dryer or a heating pad. Never use a flame. If the pipe is already cracked or water is spilling, shut off the main valve first.

The American Red Cross gives the same steps, and this guide adds what Georgia homes need. You will find a safe thawing routine, a table of the places pipes freeze in this area, an insurance paperwork list, and a prevention checklist for the next cold night.

Confirm the freeze and find the pipe

A faucet that gives only a trickle, or nothing at all, is the classic sign. The Red Cross says to check pipes against exterior walls and where the water service enters through the foundation. Look in the crawlspace, the garage, under sinks on outside walls, and at the hose bib.

Check every other faucet in the house too. If one pipe freezes, others may be frozen as well. Note any bulge, frost, or wet spot on a pipe, since a split pipe changes what you do next.

How to thaw a frozen pipe safely

  1. Open the faucet served by the frozen pipe, so water can flow as the ice melts and relieve pressure.
  2. Warm the pipe with an electric heating pad wrapped around it, an electric hair dryer, or towels soaked in hot water. A space heater works if it sits well away from anything flammable.
  3. Keep heating the section until full water pressure returns.
  4. Check the other faucets and the pipe for leaks as the ice melts.
  5. If you cannot find the frozen spot, cannot reach it, or cannot thaw it, call a licensed plumber.

What never to use

The Red Cross warns against a blowtorch, a kerosene or propane heater, a charcoal stove, or any open flame device on a frozen pipe. Fire is the obvious risk.

Skip antifreeze in pool or sprinkler lines unless the manufacturer or installer says to use it. The Red Cross notes that antifreeze is harmful to people, pets, wildlife, and landscaping.

If the pipe has already burst

The Insurance Information Institute puts the first step plainly. If your pipes burst, turn off the water. Know where the main shutoff is before you need it, and test that it turns. Then protect your valuables, stay clear of any room where water reaches outlets or appliances, and call an electrician or emergency line if you are unsure.

Dry the area properly, because water left in walls and floors invites mold. A split section needs a real repair, not tape. Our burst pipe repair team can cut out the damaged run and replace it, and you can call (770) 214-4545 any hour for emergency help.

Photos, receipts, and what to tell your insurer

The Insurance Information Institute says a standard homeowners policy is likely to cover most deep-freeze disasters and tells owners to call their insurance professional as soon as possible. Policies differ on details, so ask what yours requires. Good records make any claim easier.

  • Photos and video of the frozen or split pipe and every wet area, taken before cleanup begins.
  • A list of damaged items with the age and rough value of each.
  • Receipts for emergency repairs, hotel stays, fans, and dehumidifiers.
  • The plumber's written note on what failed and where.
  • The damaged pipe section, if the plumber can hand it over.
  • The date and time you found the problem and when you shut off the water.

Renters should send the same photos to the landlord in writing and keep copies. Tenants may also want to ask their own renter's insurer what it covers.

Where Georgia homes tend to freeze

These locations come straight from the Red Cross list of pipes that freeze most often. In a Georgia house, the vulnerable run is usually a short stretch that never sees heat, such as the line to a back hose bib.

A prevention checklist for the next cold night

  • Let cold water drip from a faucet served by exposed pipes. Running water, even a trickle, helps keep pipes from freezing.
  • Keep the thermostat at the same setting day and night, and set it no lower than 55 degrees if you leave town.
  • Wrap exposed lines in foam sleeves, or use UL-listed heat tape or cable following the maker's directions.
  • Leave cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls.
  • Know where the main shutoff is and make sure the valve turns.
  • Drain pool and sprinkler lines using the maker's directions.

None of these steps costs much, and the Red Cross points out that a higher heating bill for a few nights beats a much more expensive repair. Do the work in the fall, when the weather is mild enough to work in the crawlspace.

Rentals, vacant homes, and trips out of town

Roughly six in ten Union City households rent, so many frozen pipe calls start with a tenant. Tell the landlord in writing as soon as you see a trickle, and attach photos. The owner decides who does the repair, and a clear paper trail protects you if damage spreads.

Vacant homes freeze first, because no one is there to notice a trickle. The Red Cross says to leave the heat on at no lower than 55 degrees when you travel in cold weather. The Insurance Information Institute also suggests asking a neighbor to check the house regularly while you are away.

After the thaw, check faucets and fittings

A pipe can freeze without splitting and still leave damage at fittings, valves, and faucets. After the ice melts, run every tap and look under each sink for drips. A faucet that now drips or a cracked supply line may need a faucet repair before it turns into a larger leak.

Homes in Fairburn and Palmetto share the same exposure, since the freeze risk comes from how a house is built and where its pipes run. If water pressure stays weak after a long thaw, there may be another frozen section you have not found.
