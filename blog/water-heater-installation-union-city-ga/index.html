Water Heater Installation in Union City, GA, DIY or Hire a Plumber?

A correct water heater installation joins water, fuel or power, venting, and safety devices so the tank runs safely for years. Most Union City homeowners should hire a licensed plumber for gas heaters, power-vent heaters, and every tankless unit. Mistakes on those jobs involve carbon monoxide, gas leaks, and scalding water.

This guide is for the buyer who is still deciding. It covers what a good install includes, which pieces you can handle, how to choose between tank, tankless, and heat pump, and how install day runs. If your heater has already failed and you only need it swapped, the water heater replacement page covers that route.

What a correct water heater installation includes

A water heater is more than a tank on a stand. The install is a set of connections and safety devices, and the whole system is only as safe as its weakest piece. Manufacturer manuals list the same core items, so use this list when you read a quote.

  • Shutoff and connections. A working cold-water shutoff on the inlet and new connectors or pipe on the hot and cold ports, with dielectric unions or couplings where the tank meets copper.
  • Relief valve. A listed temperature and pressure relief valve threaded straight into the tank opening, with no other valve between it and the tank.
  • Discharge pipe. A full-size pipe off the relief valve that slopes down and ends within 6 inches of a floor drain or outside the building. It is never capped, plugged, or reduced.
  • Expansion control. A thermal expansion tank when the home has a closed water system, which happens when a backflow preventer or check valve sits at the meter.
  • Drain pan. A metal pan piped to a drain where a leak could stain a ceiling or ruin a floor.
  • Gas work. A gas line with a sediment trap, a soapy-water leak test on every joint, a vent that suits the model, and enough combustion air.
  • Fill and purge. A full tank before power or flame, then a hot faucet opened for about 3 minutes to push out air and sediment.

Can you install a water heater yourself?

Some owners can swap a plain electric tank heater, but plenty should not try. A.O. Smith's gas heater manual says that anyone not qualified, and licensed or certified as the local authority requires, should not attempt the procedures in it. Rinnai's tankless manual says a trained professional must install the unit and leak test it, and that improper installation voids the warranty.

Tank, tankless, or heat pump, which type fits your home

The US Department of Energy puts the life of a storage tank heater at about 10 to 15 years and a tankless unit at about 20 years. Tankless output tops out around 2 to 5 gallons per minute, so a busy household may need more than one unit. Heat pump models also last about 10 to 15 years.

Ask three questions before you pick. How many fixtures run at once on your busiest morning? Where will the unit sit, and can it vent from there? Does the gas line already reach, and is it sized for the load?

Renting in Union City, who decides on the heater

Census QuickFacts shows that only 40.4 percent of Union City homes are owner-occupied, so roughly six in ten households rent. In a rental, a failed heater usually starts with the tenant and ends with the landlord. Report it in writing, and do not buy or install a replacement without the owner's approval.

The owner picks the type and the contractor. That matters because the city says permits are issued to the certified contractor, so a tenant or handy neighbor cannot simply pull one. If the heater is leaking or you smell gas, call the landlord and the gas utility at once and leave the repair to professionals.

Install day, step by step

  1. The plumber confirms the model, fuel type, vent path, and shutoff valves before touching anything.
  2. Power or gas is switched off, the cold supply is closed, and the old tank is drained to a floor drain or outdoors.
  3. The old heater is disconnected and removed, and the spot is checked for rust, water stains, and a soft floor.
  4. The new heater is set level, on a pan where needed, and the water lines, relief valve, discharge pipe, and expansion tank go in.
  5. Gas or electrical connections and venting are completed, and gas joints are tested for leaks.
  6. The tank is filled, air is purged through a hot faucet, and then power is restored or the burner is lit.
  7. The plumber checks the vent draft, tests the discharge path, and shows you where the shutoffs are.

A swap that reuses a sound vent and good shutoffs is the simplest case. Moving from a tank to a tankless unit, or running a new vent, adds gas and vent work and makes for a longer job. Warning signs of a poor install

Stop and call a professional if you see any of these

A rotten-egg gas smell, a hiss at a gas joint, or a carbon monoxide alarm. Leave the house, then call 911 or the gas utility from outside.

Water dripping or steaming from the relief valve discharge pipe. Rheem's valve instructions say discharge at the drain outlet points to an abnormal condition in the heater or a defective valve, and a qualified service person should find the cause.

A pool of water under the tank, or a drain pan that keeps filling. Close the cold-water shutoff and call.

Questions to ask before you approve any quote

  • Will the vent, gas line, and shutoff valves be reused or replaced, and why?
  • Does the quote include a new relief valve and a discharge pipe, and where does the pipe end?
  • Is my water system closed, and does it need an expansion tank?
  • Who pulls the permit if one is needed, and what inspection follows?
  • Is the old heater hauled away, and what does the manufacturer warranty require from the installer?
  • Will the full price be clear before work starts?

These questions work the same way for homes in nearby Fairburn and South Fulton, so use them with any contractor you compare. A good answer is specific and names parts. A vague answer is a reason to keep asking.

Which codes apply

Water heater installs are covered by the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and, for gas units, the International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC). Union City's permit page lists both in the 2018 editions with Georgia amendments. Ask Community Development at (770) 515-7920 whether your swap needs a permit.
